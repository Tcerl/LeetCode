### Passages 15–17 — Feedback-method reference, email & reply (186–190)

**Document 1 — Reference**

**CUSTOMER FEEDBACK METHODS**

| Method | Sample size | Depth | Speed | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| In-app survey (1–2 questions) | Very large | Shallow | Real-time | Tracking a metric over time; spotting a sudden drop |
| Email survey (longer) | Large | Medium | Days | Understanding "why" behind a trend; segmenting by customer type |
| 1:1 interviews (30–45 min) | Small (6–12) | Deep | Weeks | Discovering problems you didn't know to ask about |
| Support-ticket analysis | Whatever comes in | Medium | Ongoing | Finding friction points; free, already collected |

Start with what you already have (tickets), use surveys to size a problem, and use interviews to understand one you can't explain.

**Document 2 — Email**

**Document 3 — Reply**

**From:** research@company.example **To:** Owen Reyes **Subject:** RE: Drop in trial conversions

Hi Owen,

Do this in order. First, I'll pull the support tickets from trial users over the last quarter — that's free and might show an obvious friction point (a broken step, a confusing screen) in a day.

If that's not conclusive, we send a short email survey to users whose trials expired without converting, asking the one open question "what stopped you from subscribing?" That'll size the top reasons within a week.

If the survey answers are vague, we book six interviews with recent non-converters to dig in. That's two to three weeks, but it's how we'd find a problem we don't currently have words for.

I'd resist a big interview round as step one — it's slow and you might spend it on the wrong question.
